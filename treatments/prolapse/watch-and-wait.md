# Watch and Wait for Prolapse

For many women with pelvic organ prolapse, especially in mild cases, you may not need treatment right away. "Watch and wait," or watchful waiting, is a valid way to manage it. Left alone, most prolapse stays about the same: in one study, 78% of women with symptomatic, untreated prolapse had no change at 16 months, and mild prolapse sometimes improves on its own.

## What is Watch and Wait?

You and your doctor keep track of your prolapse over time without treating it right away. This is often recommended when:

- Your prolapse is mild
- You have minimal or no symptoms
- Your quality of life is not significantly affected

## Who is a Good Candidate?

You might be a good candidate for watchful waiting if:

- Your prolapse doesn't extend beyond the opening of your vagina
- You're not experiencing discomfort or pain
- The prolapse doesn't interfere with your daily activities or sexual function
- You prefer to avoid treatment for now

## Benefits of Watchful Waiting

1. Avoids unnecessary treatment
2. Gives you time to see if symptoms progress or resolve on their own
3. Gives you a chance to try conservative measures like pelvic floor exercises
4. Spares you the risks of more invasive treatments

## What to Expect

During the watchful waiting period:

- You'll tell your doctor about any new or worsening symptoms
- You may learn pelvic floor exercises to help support your pelvic organs
- Your doctor may recommend lifestyle changes to keep the prolapse from getting worse

## Self-Care During Watchful Waiting

Things you can do yourself in the meantime:

- Practice pelvic floor exercises regularly
- Maintain a healthy weight
- Use good body mechanics when lifting
- Treat chronic cough or constipation
- Stay physically active

## When to Consider Other Treatments

It may be time to look at other treatments if:

- Your symptoms worsen
- The prolapse progresses
- You start experiencing pain or discomfort
- You have trouble emptying your bladder or bowel
- Your quality of life suffers

## Making the Decision

Whether to watch and wait is your call, made together with your doctor. Good questions to ask:

- What signs should I watch for?
- How often should I have check-ups?
- What can I do to prevent the prolapse from worsening?

Watchful waiting still means paying attention to changes, so you can move to treatment if and when you need it.
