# Can fecal incontinence be cured?

Most women with fecal incontinence get much better, and many get to where leakage no longer gets in the way of daily life. Which treatment fits you depends on what's causing it.

## Treatment options

First-line treatments:
- Diet changes: fiber supplements to firm up your stool, and cutting out foods that trigger leakage
- Pelvic floor physical therapy with biofeedback, which strengthens the anal sphincter and teaches the muscles to work together
- Anti-diarrheal medicine such as loperamide (Imodium) to firm up loose stool

If those aren't enough:
- Sacral neuromodulation, sometimes called a "bowel pacemaker." A small implanted device stimulates the nerves that control your bowel. The FDA approved it for fecal incontinence in 2011, success rates are high, and it can work even if you have a sphincter defect.
- Sphincter repair surgery. We rarely recommend it now. The repair tends to break down over the years, recovery is painful, and infection rates are high.

## Dr. Stewart's perspective

"The approach depends on what's causing the problem. If the nerves are the issue, sacral neuromodulation is excellent. It's one of the most satisfying treatments I offer, because the improvement is often dramatic. For many women, pelvic floor therapy and dietary management provide substantial relief. I almost never recommend sphincter repair because the long-term results just aren't there."
