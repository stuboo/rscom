# How long is recovery from prolapse surgery?

Recovery from prolapse surgery depends on the procedure, but most women are back to normal activities in about 4 to 6 weeks. Many go home the same day. In a 2026 study, about half of women felt recovered 2 weeks after minimally invasive prolapse surgery, and 93% felt recovered by 6 weeks. Women who had a vaginal repair tended to bounce back a little sooner than women who had robotic sacrocolpopexy.

## The first week

The first few days are usually the most uncomfortable. You may notice:

- Mild to moderate pelvic soreness and pressure
- Some vaginal spotting or discharge
- Tiredness while your body heals
- Constipation from anesthesia and pain medicine

We use a multimodal pain approach: several non-opioid treatments together (like ibuprofen, acetaminophen, and ice packs), so most patients need little or no narcotic pain medicine. About 30 to 40% of patients take no opioids at all after going home. Those who do need a few pills mostly use them in the first 2 to 3 days.

Constipation is common after any pelvic surgery. I usually recommend MiraLAX and possibly a gentle laxative. Drinking plenty of fluids and walking also keep your bowels moving.

Walk from the first day, a little farther each day, and rest when you are tired.

## Weeks 2 to 4

Most women feel noticeably better during this stretch:

- Pain decreases day by day
- Energy comes back gradually
- Daily tasks become comfortable
- Driving usually resumes at 1 to 2 weeks, once you are off narcotics and can wear a seatbelt

Some women have trouble emptying their bladder in the first days after surgery. This is called urinary retention, and it happens after 15 to 45% of pelvic reconstructive surgeries. It is temporary. If it happens to you, you may go home with a catheter for a few days or learn to empty your bladder with a small catheter yourself, and we will check again until you are emptying well on your own.

## Activity and lifting

Many women are told not to lift anything heavier than a gallon of milk for weeks. That advice is common, but there is little evidence for it. Coughing, having a bowel movement, and getting up from a chair put as much pressure on the pelvic floor as lifting or exercise. In the 2026 study above, women had no limits on lifting, running, or sit-ups after surgery, and none had a serious complication in the first 6 weeks.

The main restriction is pelvic rest, which means nothing in the vagina (no sex, tampons, or douching) for at least 6 weeks. For everything else, go at your own pace. Start light, build up, and if something hurts, stop and try again in a few days.

## Weeks 4 to 6

This is when most women feel ready to get back to their routine:

- Return to work (desk jobs sooner, physical jobs later)
- Exercise picks back up toward your usual level

## After 6 weeks

At your postoperative visit I check how the vagina has healed. Once it has, you can have sex again. The first few times may feel different, and going slowly with a lubricant helps.

Full tissue healing takes about 3 months, and that is when your final results become clear. I tell my patients that 6 weeks is a turning point, but the full benefit of surgery really shows at 3 months. In a large national registry, 93% of women who had prolapse surgery said they felt much better or very much better at 1 year, and 94% at 3 years. Only about 2% needed another treatment.
